\tightsection{1. Objective and approach}

The challenge represents a public health emergency. Our robot handles two jobs. It delivers ten medical kits in groups of 2, 6, and 2. It also carries and releases two wooden beams for the quarantine zone. These tasks move supplies and barriers without a person entering the field during the round.

The robot starts completely inside the official $480 \times 280$ mm starting zone. A team member switches it on and presses one button. From that point, the ESP32 runs the full route. The robot uses an ultrasonic sensor for wall distance, two downward-facing IR sensors for a black tape checkpoint, and calibrated turn timing. It stops after the final beam release.

\note{\textbf{One competition program:} The submitted robot uses \texttt{MinimalistCareBot.ino}. The other sketches in the repository are kept for hardware checks, fault testing, and development. They are not extra operating modes for the competition robot.}

\tightsection{Tasks completed by the program}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Drive to the first wall checkpoint, turn left, and release two kits.
  \item Detect the black tape checkpoint and release six kits.
  \item Drive to the next wall checkpoint and release the other two kits.
  \item Turn toward the quarantine area and stop at the next wall checkpoint.
  \item Release the first beam, turn left, release the second beam, and stop.
\end{enumerate}

The code releases complete preloaded groups. It does not count individual kits. This keeps the mechanism basic and reduces the number of moving parts.

\tightsection{2. Physical robot}

The official robot is the four-wheel machine shown in the photographs on the next page. It has a flat wooden chassis, four rubber wheels, three open kit compartments, several wooden supports, and servo-operated mechanisms. The front ultrasonic sensor is visible on its wooden bracket. Electronics and wiring sit in the centre of the chassis.

The photographs show four geared drive units, one at each wheel. The program still controls two drive sides. The two left motors act as one left-side pair, and the two right motors act as one right-side pair. The final motor driver must safely handle the combined startup and stall current of each pair.

Three visible top-mounted servos operate the kit sections. Two more servo channels are reserved for the beam-release mechanisms. The supplied images do not show the downward IR sensors or every beam linkage clearly, so their final brackets must be checked on the actual robot before the run.

\textbf{Size and mass.} The supplied photographs do not provide a reliable scale or mass reading. The complete robot, including loaded kits and beams, is checked as one assembly against the $480 \times 280$ mm starting zone.

\tightsection{Kit and beam mechanisms}

The three bins are loaded with 2, 6, and 2 wooden kits. Each kit is $25 \times 25 \times 20$ mm. Servo channels 0, 1, and 2 open the three release mechanisms. Gravity drops each complete group while the robot is stopped.

Two holders carry the quarantine beams. Each beam has a $60 \times 20$ mm section. Servo channel 3 releases the first beam, and channel 4 releases the second. The program turns left between the two releases. The holder positions must allow each beam to leave the robot cleanly and stand in the required location.

The PCA9685 VCC pin uses ESP32 3.3 V for logic. Its V+ pin uses a separate regulated servo supply. All grounds are joined. If the ultrasonic ECHO signal is 5 V, a 10 k$\Omega$ and 15 k$\Omega$ divider lowers it before GPIO34. The four motors must not overload the L298N. If their measured combined current is too high, the same ESP32 control signals must drive a higher-current dual-channel motor driver.

\tightsection{Power}

The motors use a suitable battery through the motor driver. The ESP32 uses a regulated board input. The five servos use a regulated 5 to 6 V supply through PCA9685 V+. Battery capacity and regulator current must be selected from measured motor and servo current, especially while all four motors start or turn together.

\tightsection{5. Autonomous working and calculations}

On power-up, the motors remain off. The ESP32 initializes the PCA9685 at address \texttt{0x40}, sets the five servo channels to their closed positions, and waits for the GPIO32 button. One press starts the route below.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Move forward until the ultrasonic reading is 20 cm or less.
  \item Stop, turn 90 degrees left, and open channel 0 for the first two kits.
  \item Move until both IR sensors detect the black tape stripe. Stop and open channel 1 for six kits.
  \item Continue to the next 20 cm wall reading. Stop and open channel 2 for the other two kits.
  \item Turn left, move to the next 20 cm wall reading, and stop.
  \item Turn left and open channel 3 for the first beam.
  \item Turn left again, open channel 4 for the second beam, and stop permanently.
\end{enumerate}

The robot is autonomous after the button press. The sensors end the forward legs, and a calibrated time controls each turn. The program stops if an ultrasonic echo disappears, a wall is reached before the tape checkpoint, or a movement exceeds its timeout.

\tightsection{Motor speed estimate}

The program uses an 8-bit PWM value of 102:
\[
\text{duty cycle}=\frac{102}{255}\times100\%=40\%.
\]
For a motor rated at 500 RPM, $500\times0.40=200$ RPM is only a starting estimate. Motor speed is not perfectly proportional to PWM, especially with four motors and a loaded chassis. The real wheel speed must be measured.

If wheel diameter is $D$ mm and measured speed is $n$ RPM, forward speed is
\[
v=\frac{\pi Dn}{60}\ \text{mm/s}.
\]

\tightsection{Ultrasonic distance and turning}

For echo time $t$ in microseconds, the distance is
\[
d=\frac{t\times0.0343}{2}\ \text{cm}.
\]
The 20 cm threshold is measured from the sensor face. Wheel coasting and the front bracket reduce the actual free space. Left turns currently use 580 ms of opposite wheel motion. That value must be adjusted with the loaded four-wheel chassis because skid steering depends on tyre grip, weight, and battery voltage.
