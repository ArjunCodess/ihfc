The route

Optional lane correction and measured stopping clearances are omitted for clarity. The beams must be mounted to land correctly from one final stop.

What happens at each stop

  1. First two kits. The ultrasonic sensor detects the left wall. The robot turns down and opens bin A.
  2. Six kits. The rear IR detects the black crossing. A calibrated offset positions the middle bin before it opens.
  3. Last two kits. The robot stops near the bottom wall and opens bin B.
  4. Both beams. It turns rightward, reaches the final wall-distance target and opens both grippers.

There is no closing cycle during the mission and no reverse movement after dropping the beams.

How it is controlled

An ESP32 commands the L298N motor driver and five positional servos. The ultrasonic sensor measures wall distance; the rear IR identifies a crossing. Turns use calibrated timing. The bins are preloaded, so the code releases groups rather than counting individual kits.

What has been checked

The source compiles with Arduino-ESP32 3.3.5. Tests cover the complete route, open-only releases, missed echoes, marker faults, motor-control failures and stopping. Motor electrical ratings, servo loads and real landing accuracy still need physical verification.
